Calamity

James Barker

Edgar Casey has lost his marriage and come to Moscow hoping, perhaps, to lose himself as well. Instead, he finds Calamity.

A bar devoted to Doris Day, Calamity is part sanctuary, part madhouse, a place where appearances are everything and nothing is quite what it seems. Beneath its theatrical excesses, an extraordinary collection of people have made a home for themselves, each negotiating the difficult business of becoming who they really are.

At the centre of it all is Mary.

Then there is Honey.

Honey has questions about Mary. Mary has secrets. And before Edgar quite knows what is happening, he finds himself helping Honey uncover a past that Mary has spent years keeping to herself.

What begins in Moscow will take them further than any of them expects, through friendship and betrayal, sex and religion, performance and reinvention, and into the strange territory between who we are, who we pretend to be, and who the world allows us to become.

There will be arguments. There will be revelations. There will be vodka.

And somewhere along the way, Edgar may discover that losing yourself and finding yourself are not so very different after all.
